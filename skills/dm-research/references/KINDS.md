# Kinds

Only the Lead reads this file, at the brief and again at the options step. A run has one primary kind (`kind:` in `state.md`), which sets what an option is, the report's kind-specific sections, and the recommendation's shape. Frameworks are offered, not mandated: use one only when it would move a research question or a score.

## opportunity — business opportunity research

- **Options are** ways to enter or positions to take: segments, wedges, business models, build / partner / acquire, and always *don't enter* (`OPT-00`).
- **Questions worth asking:** Who has the problem, how badly, and what do they do today? How big is the reachable market, built bottom-up (customers × price) and checked top-down? Who competes, including substitutes and doing nothing? Why now: what changed? What is the right to win? What do unit economics look like at small scale? What kills it?
- **Frameworks to consider:** bottom-up market sizing, jobs-to-be-done, the five forces, a competitor table on the criteria customers use, unit economics.
- **Report adds:** §Market (size with derivation), §Customer and problem, §Competition and substitutes, §Why now, §Economics.
- **Recommendation shape:** go, no-go, or which wedge first; plus the **cheapest test** that would validate or kill it before real money is spent, and its pass/fail threshold, set at a price and volume that reach the brief's goal; a pass below it is labelled "side business", not "go". Check that the test's window and sample can settle what it claims to settle; say what it measures and what it can't.

## decision — important decision research

- **Options are** the choices on the table, the status quo, and at least one option that reframes the choice (split it, sequence it, buy time).
- **Questions worth asking:** what each option really costs (money, time, attention, foreclosed options); its realistic worst case and how recoverable it is; what evidence exists from people who made each choice; which assumption the choice is most sensitive to.
- **Lenses:** `dm-decide`'s, scaled to reversibility (its lens-count table): two-way doors 2–3, one-way doors all seven.
- **Report adds:** §Reversibility and stakes, §Guardrails (what to watch after committing), §Reversal plan.
- **Recommendation shape:** the choice; "a different option wins if"; guardrails; the reversal plan and its trigger.

## exploratory — understand a landscape

- **Options are** directions worth pursuing next: areas to go deeper on, positions to take, bets to place; the status quo is *stop here*.
- **Questions worth asking:** What are the main approaches or schools of thought, and where do they disagree? Who are the key players, and what are they betting on? What is settled, contested, and unknown? Where is the activity moving?
- **Frameworks to consider:** a landscape map (approaches × maturity), a timeline of key shifts, a settled / contested / unknown table.
- **Report adds:** §Landscape map, §Settled · contested · unknown, §Who to watch.
- **Recommendation shape:** where to focus and why; the next three questions worth paying to answer, each with what answering it would unlock.

## possible — art of the possible

- **Options are** concepts: things that could be built or done, graded by evidence, from conservative to ambitious; the status quo is *wait and watch*.
- **Questions worth asking:** What is demonstrated today (shipping, reproducible), what is claimed (demo, paper, announcement), and what is speculative? What do analogues in other domains show? What is the blocking constraint for each concept, and when might it lift? Build, buy, or wait?
- **Evidence grade:** every concept carries `demonstrated | claimed | speculative`, from its cited claims; a `claimed` grade needs the claim's source to be the party making it, labelled.
- **Frameworks to consider:** a capability frontier (what is possible now vs. in 1 and 3 years), value × feasibility × time-to-feasible, analogue mining.
- **Report adds:** §Frontier (demonstrated · claimed · speculative), §Concepts, §Signposts.
- **Recommendation shape:** what to prototype first and what it would prove; the **signposts** (observable events) that would pull a speculative concept forward or kill it.

## Mixed questions

When a question spans kinds (an opportunity that turns on what's possible), pick the kind whose recommendation shape the decider needs, and borrow research questions from the other.
